# How Processors/Handlers Work in the Turbo-Suslik System

[Book contents](../README.md) | [Part contents](README.md)

The Turbo-Suslik system has been in widespread use since 2008. Over this time, it has been applied by over 100,000 of people to solve practical psychological tasks: working with emotional reactions, persistent states, automatic behavioral patterns, and internal conflicts. Over the years of practice, the method has been repeatedly revised, simplified, and refined. Everything that did not produce stable results was discarded. Everything that worked reliably across a wide range of people was retained and reduced to a minimal working form.

In its current form, this is not a theory, not a philosophy, and not “belief in the right words.” It is a clear technical algorithm whose shape is defined not by speculation, but by accumulated practical use. We do not proceed from assumptions about how it “should work.” We proceed from what, in practice, produces the most stable results with minimal involvement of conscious control.

That is why what follows in the book is not a set of interpretations, but working principles and procedures. Your task is not to agree or argue, but to follow the protocol as described and evaluate the results directly.

**How This Thing Works - A Simple Explanation**

The Turbo-Suslik system is based on a simple principle: many internal problems exist not because “something is wrong” with a person, but because contradictory automatic reactions are operating simultaneously inside. One part pulls in one direction, another in the opposite. The conscious mind tries to resolve this, but usually only makes it worse: tension appears, getting stuck, recurring patterns, and the familiar feeling of “I understand everything, but I still act the same way.”

The system’s protocols are designed to remove this internal conflict **without analysis, without thinking, and without control.** You simply indicate what needs to be worked on, launch the processing routine, and the process continues automatically in the background. You do not need to understand how it works - you only need to observe the result in changes in reactions and behavior.

You simply point your attention (or write down on paper) the “material” and assign the subconscious the task of processing it by speaking a trigger word. And then you go make tea. That’s literally it. In practice, this is one of the simplest formats of work that exists.

If you are looking for fast and practical changes, this explanation is more than enough. The rest can be skipped, and you can move straight to practice (the chapter on polarities).

## How Processors Work: Instructions, Context, and Activation

For anyone who has been working with AI (like ChatGPT), the simplest and most practically accurate analogy is prompting. A prompt is a detailed instruction we give AI in order to get a particular response. That is broadly what we are doing here as well: using a precise instruction to specify how the “AI” in your head should act.

Every operation in the system has four elements:

**material** - whatever you present to the system: a situation, thought, emotion, memory, bodily reaction, or inner conflict;

**protocol** - a detailed instruction defining what counts as material, what should be done with it, and according to which rules;

**trigger word** - a short activation command;

**processor** - an automatic background process that carries out the instruction already given.

A **protocol** is not a magic formula or a collection of “correct words.” It is a detailed technical description of a task for the automatic part of the mind. It specifies in advance which material should be accepted for processing, what should be done with it, which related reactions should be taken into account, and what outcome the process should move toward.

The **trigger word** does not contain the entire instruction by itself. It works like a short Run command: it calls up the full protocol that has already been read and put into operation.

## Where does a processor come from?

A processor is not handed to you by the author of the method, and it is not created out of nothing when you read the text. Human beings already have automatic mechanisms that constantly process experience: they restructure links between events and reactions, reduce the significance of some stimuli, reinforce others, allow unreinforced reactions to fade, and change habitual ways of responding to situations.

In ordinary life, these processes run by themselves: slowly, unevenly, and often in a direction the person would not consciously consider useful. The Turbo-Suslik system does not create a new mechanism. It gives existing mechanisms a clearly specified task. That is why a processor can be understood as a preconfigured mode of automatic operation. It is not a separate entity. It is simply the name for a process that is activated by a particular instruction and applied to particular material.

## What does “loading” a processor mean?

“Loading” is a conventional term. You are not downloading a program into your head or teaching yourself a new skill in the usual sense. You are introducing the automatic system to a complete specification: this is what will count as material, this is what must be done with that material, and this is the command that will activate the work.

After that, the trigger word becomes a short call to the entire instruction. This is why the protocol is read once: you do not need to repeat the long text every time. You are no longer rewriting the task. You are launching a process that has already been specified.

## How is it activated?

The sequence is simple:

1. You notice or write down the material.
1. You hold it in attention just long enough for the system to understand what it is working with.
1. You say the trigger word.
1. The full, previously loaded protocol is called up automatically.

The rest of the work runs in the background, without analysis, supervision, or attempts to “do it right.” Consciousness does not carry out the processing manually. Its only job is to indicate the material and start the process. Everything else happens automatically.

It is like assigning a task to a system that can work with its own internal material. You do not have to control every stage of execution. You only need to specify clearly enough what should be processed and under which protocol.

## The role of intention

Intention is not strong desire, belief, or emotional buildup. It is the point at which you actually hand the task over for processing and do not immediately try to take control back.

You have given the command. There is no need to stand over the system asking: “Is it working already? How exactly is it working? Is it making a mistake?” Constantly checking the process simply returns you to the familiar habit of manually chewing over the same material.

A protocol does not override the mind’s basic constraints or give you imaginary “root access” to yourself. But within its defined scope, it initiates the automatic processing of the material you have presented.

The result may appear as a reduction in emotional charge, an end to feeling stuck, a change in a habitual reaction, greater freedom of choice, or the loss of a subject’s former significance. The event or thought itself does not have to disappear. What changes first and foremost is the way your system responds to it.

## Why is the protocol text fixed?

The text is fixed not because words have mystical power. It is fixed because it is the complete technical specification of the process. Every part of the instruction defines the scope of work, the conditions, the boundaries, and the way the material is handled. If you decide to “improve” the wording, shorten the text, or replace words with more elegant ones, you may silently alter the task itself.

The protocol does not require creative adaptation. First, use it exactly as written and see how it works. It only makes sense to modify an instruction when you understand precisely which part of the process you are changing and why. For effective work, however, nothing needs to be changed. Everything already works out of the box, tested over more than eighteen years of practice by more than 100,000 users.

**And a few words about wording.** As mentioned earlier, instructions for the subconscious use “we,” “our,” and similar forms instead of “I,” “my.” This is not a typo, not a mistake, and not a reference to any collective. As already stated, an “ordinary person” is usually fragmented to some degree. A unified “I” is rare, while there is typically a whole set of different “parts of the subconscious,” “subpersonalities,” “aspects of personality,” and other fragments.

That is why the instruction uses the form “we” - it refers precisely to the totality of all these parts, which ideally should function as a single whole. In this way, we signal to the subconscious that it should process **all parts, subpersonalities, aspects, and so on**, including those that are dissociated or fragmented.
